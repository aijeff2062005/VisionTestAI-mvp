# DIY single-eye occluder (carton)

You do **not** need a large clinical paddle. Cut a small disc from a carton box.

## Recommended size (adult, one eye)

| Dimension | Value | Why |
|-----------|-------|-----|
| **Disc diameter** | **55–65 mm** (~**6 cm**) | Covers adult orbital opening (~40×35 mm) with margin; blocks peeking |
| Minimum usable | ~50 mm | May leak light at edges if held loosely |
| Max for “small” DIY | ~70 mm | Still one-eye; larger becomes a full paddle |
| Thickness | 1–3 mm carton | Opaque; double-layer if light leaks |
| Optional handle | 15×80 mm tab | Hold without fingers in front of the other eye |

```
      _________
     /         \      ← disc Ø 60 mm
    |     ·     |     · = centre over closed/covered eye
     \_________/
          |
          |  ← optional tab / stick
```

## How to use (BQP-aligned)

- Cover **one** eye with the disc (not the palm).
- Keep **both eyes open** behind the cover (don’t squeeze shut the covered eye if possible).
- Patient at **5 m**; disc held flush, no peek gap.

## Cut steps

1. Draw a **60 mm** circle on carton.
2. Cut out; optional: glue two layers.
3. Optional: cut a short handle tab.
4. Paint matte black/dark on the patient-facing side to cut reflections.

Child / small face: start at **50 mm**. Very large adult orbits: up to **65–70 mm**.
